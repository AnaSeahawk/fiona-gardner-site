Intention, Consciousness and Geometry

I have been exploring a new dimension of my work with urine, bringing together intention, visualization, consciousness, and the geometric patterns that become visible under the microscope.

In these experiments, a particular state of consciousness is entered and a general intention is held and directed toward the urine. The aim is not to produce a specific shape, colour or outcome. The intention is established, the experience is allowed to unfold, and then what becomes visible is observed, with each sample compared against a control.

The first experiment held a heart-focused intention, the second joy, and the third a connection with the higher self and the energy of the Divine Mother. Each represents a different quality of consciousness: the heart speaks to connection, joy to a particular state of being, and the Divine Mother to a nurturing and universal presence. The focus is on whether differences appear in the fluid's geometry, colour, structure or overall patterning under each condition.

The patterns are observed without deciding in advance what they should look like or what they mean. The control, repeated experiments and consistent preparation help distinguish recurring differences from natural variation.

This work extends my exploration of a geometric healing code: an inquiry into the relationship between naturally occurring forms, awareness and our experience of wholeness. Introducing intention adds another dimension. The fluid becomes the medium, geometry the visible expression, and the microscope reveals details that would otherwise remain unseen. Consciousness itself becomes part of the question.

The central question I continue to explore is this:

When we enter a particular state of consciousness and direct an intention, is there a relationship between that experience and the physical patterns we subsequently observe?

For now, it remains an open question. The intention is to remain an observer, allowing the patterns to emerge without imposing an explanation, and documenting what these observations may reveal over time.
