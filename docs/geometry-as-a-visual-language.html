Geometry as a Visual Language

I have been exploring the geometric patterns that emerge when urine is observed under the microscope, and I am increasingly drawn to the relationship between these patterns, the human visual system, and the possibility of geometry as a visual language of healing.

Nature is full of geometry: spirals, circles, branching networks and radial forms. These patterns are not only found in the world around us; they also emerge within fluids under magnification. What fascinates me is the moment something ordinarily unseen becomes visible. A fluid reveals a pattern, the pattern becomes an image, and the eye recognises it. From the retina, that image travels to the brain, where shape, colour, symmetry and contrast meet memory, emotion and personal experience.

Mandalas offer an intriguing parallel. Across contemplative traditions, their concentric circles and central points have drawn the eye inward and outward as a focus for contemplation and a symbol of wholeness. The geometry I observe in urine is not deliberately constructed in this way; it emerges through the changing behaviour of the fluid itself. Yet it can evoke similar qualities of symmetry, organisation and interconnectedness.

This is where my idea of a geometric healing code begins. For me, the term is not a claim that a particular shape produces a specific biological outcome. It is an invitation to explore the relationship between form, perception, awareness and the experience of wholeness, and what these patterns may offer when brought into conscious observation. It connects with my wider exploration of water, dew and plasma, and how a fluid can reveal what might otherwise remain hidden.

The fluid becomes the medium. The geometry becomes the visible expression. The eye becomes the instrument of perception. And the observer becomes the point at which the pattern is consciously experienced.

Could these patterns become more than microscopic observations, serving, like mandalas, as focal points that invite us into a deeper relationship with the body, nature, and the animating presence that moves through everything?

Perhaps it is another way of exploring how the unseen becomes visible.
